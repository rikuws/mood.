# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

adaptive

## Users

mood. is for people who make things and want them to look like theirs: designers, developers, and founders who build with coding agents and already save what catches their eye, much of it on X. Their taste is real but scattered across years of bookmarks, screenshots, and half-remembered posts, with no clear way to say what it is or to hand it to the agent doing the building.

They save more than screens. Interiors, fashion, objects, places, type, and art shape a direction as much as interfaces do, so the collection stays open to anything. Native iOS, iPadOS, and macOS surfaces let them capture without breaking their flow, keep loose finds in General, and gather the material for something they're making into a Project.

## Product Purpose

mood. helps you find your design direction and hand it to your coding agent. It works as one loop:

1. **Collect.** Keep what you find aesthetic, with its imagery, text, author, and source, from the browser, from X, from the Share sheet, and from the bookmarks you made before mood. existed.
2. **Explore.** Start a study from work you love or from something you're making. An agent brings back real, credited work that relates to it, designs similar to it, and ways to make your own thing better.
3. **Judge.** Keep or reject each proposal and say why. Those judgments are the taste data.
4. **Distill.** Patterns are reflected back for you to confirm, then condensed into a design essence where every rule points at the references and reasons behind it.
5. **Hand off.** Export the essence as direction a coding agent can follow, then bring what it builds back and judge it against that direction.

The library stays local-first: one private library across Apple devices with optional iCloud sync, and no mood. account, API key, or model inside the app. Agents, and any services they use to search or extract, run on the person's side and only when asked.

Success means capture feels immediate and dependable, a pile of saves turns into a direction the person recognizes as their own, and an agent builds from that direction instead of from generic defaults.

## Positioning

mood. is where your design taste becomes something your agent can use. Reference libraries hand an agent what you saved; brand engines hand it someone else's system. mood. helps you work out your own direction from what you already save and how you judge it, then hands it over with receipts: every guideline traces to a reference and a reason.

## Brand Personality

Thoughtful, human, and artful. mood. should feel like a designer's working sketchbook rather than a finished gallery: collected work stays crisp, credited, and intact, while the thinking around it (questions, proposals, judgments, patterns) feels hand-made and in progress. Restrained native controls frame both. The interface is calm without making the collected world feel sterile, and the agent works on the page like a research assistant, never as a voice that takes it over.

## Anti-references

mood. should not resemble a generic Pinterest clone, a utilitarian bookmark manager, a web-style SaaS dashboard, or a chat window with a moodboard attached. It is not an image generator that invents references or fills boards with synthetic imagery, and it is not a template picker that hands people a ready-made brand. Avoid engagement-driven feeds, anonymous thumbnail grids, dense administrative chrome, and decorative interface effects that compete with saved items.

## Design Principles

1. **Your taste, with receipts.** Agents find, propose, and notice patterns; the person keeps, rejects, and confirms. Every guideline handed to an agent traces to a saved reference and, where one exists, the person's reason. Anything that can't be traced is dropped, and nothing generated enters the collection as a reference.
2. **Capture first, organize without friction.** Saving must stay fast and reliable wherever people find things, including the bookmarks they already have.
3. **Preserve why a reference mattered.** Keep authorship, source, text, imagery, notes, and verdict reasons visible enough to restore context rather than presenting bare links.
4. **Diverge, then judge.** Exploration opens wide: many real sources, and many ways to make something better, each through a named lens such as Dieter Rams' ten principles of good design and, later, the person's own rules. People narrow by judging, not by describing their taste from scratch.
5. **Let the collection lead.** Saved work, imagery, quotes, and provenance carry the personality; interface chrome stays quiet and legible.
6. **Be native where expectations matter.** Use familiar Apple navigation, controls, menus, gestures, sharing, keyboard behavior, and platform-specific layout patterns.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Follow Apple-native accessibility conventions across iOS, iPadOS, and macOS. Support Dynamic Type, meaningful VoiceOver labels, Reduce Motion, sufficient contrast, comfortable touch targets, and full keyboard navigation where appropriate on macOS. Express state through more than color or a decorative mark alone, and keep collected content usable in both light and dark appearances.
