# Concise design-essence reading

Lead with the useful interpretation, not the extraction mechanics or a JSON dump.

## Essence

One or two sentences describing the content-independent direction expressed by the source.
For a single image, explicitly say "expressed in this image." For a project, name the board
and describe the expressed direction of that selection.

## Signature tensions

Two to four contrasts that define the direction's boundaries, such as "technical but not
cold" or "strict modular order against organic imagery." Do not average a productive tension
into a generic adjective.

## Strongest invariants

The few properties most likely to survive changed copy, subject matter, workflow, and brand.
Keep each rule operational and grounded in cited visual cues.

## Preserve / avoid

Summarize the strongest `mustPreserve` and `mustAvoid` directives. Include `prefer` or
`mayVary` only when those distinctions materially help the user's next task.

## Important limitations

State missing visuals, uncertain inference, palette approximations, internally conflicting
clusters, legibility concerns, and any boundary that affects confidence.

## Provenance

For mood. sources, cite the handful of items that most strongly set the direction: title,
author or handle when present, source, and URL. Keep exact copy and brand identity out of the
portable rules.

## Source accounting

End with exactly:

```text
Source accounting: inspected: N; duplicatesRemoved: N; remoteOnlyInspected: N; skipped: N.
```

The canonical `DesignEssence` 1.0 object remains internal unless the user requests structured
output, export, downstream generation context, or machine use.
